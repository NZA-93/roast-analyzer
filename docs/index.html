Calibration (important) — the Agtron-like number is an estimate

A phone photo measures surface colour under your light; an Agtron/Lighttells meter measures near-infrared reflectance of ground or whole beans. The default mapping is a provisional linear guess. To calibrate, photograph beans whose Agtron number you know (a roaster's reference lot, or beans you measured on a meter), read their mean L* here and enter two points:

How it works
  1. Plate: bright, neutral pixels → largest region → convex hull, shrunk to skip the rim.
  2. White balance + flat-field: a plane is fitted to the plate's colour, so each bean is normalised to the white plate at its own position.
  3. Beans: saturated-brown pixels plus dark-and-wide pixels (thin contact shadows are removed by a morphological opening).
  4. Touching beans: markers from a distance transform blended with a blurred "bean body" map, then a marker-controlled watershed. Fragments are merged into neighbours, oversized blobs are split, crumbs and plate-edge pieces are rejected.
  5. Colour: median CIELAB of each bean's interior, dropping the darkest 15% (centre crease) and brightest 8% (specular highlights).
  6. Roast index: L* → Agtron-like number via your calibration; bands use SCA-style tile boundaries.
  7. Outliers: robust z-score against the whole batch (median/MAD): quaker (very pale, low chroma), underdeveloped (pale), scorched (dark).
